Projects

Handheld bioluminescence reader for therapeutic drug monitoring

MSc dissertation, University of Leeds · analogue front end and PCB · LTspice, KiCad

Complete front-end design for a handheld point-of-care reader: a Broadcom AFBR-S4N22P014M silicon photomultiplier biased at 44.5 V, an OPA657 transimpedance amplifier (30 kOhm, 1 pF), and a 16-bit ADS8866 SAR converter, powered from a single 5 V USB input.

Transimpedance 29,981 Ω flat to 6.78 MHz 2.2 pA noise floor, fully accounted for 1.59 kHz filter: noise down 55× Two detector models agree to 0.2% on charge >80 dB ambient-light spec, derived not guessed PCB DRC violations 44 → 7, zero errors

I began from the clinical requirement rather than the circuit: the therapeutic drug range set the signal level, which set the detector, which set the amplifier noise budget, and which ultimately showed a 16-bit converter would do the work a 24-bit part had been assumed for. I modelled the detector two independent ways and confirmed the microcell scaling in writing with Broadcom applications engineers. When a simulation returned near-identical outputs for three different photon rates, which is physically impossible, I traced the fault to a generic op-amp model defaulting to 10 V/µs against the datasheet's 700, corrected it, and re-ran the complete analysis set.

Honest scope: the board was designed, simulation-verified and released to fabrication files, but not manufactured within the project window, so performance figures are simulated rather than measured. Building and characterising it is exactly the work I want to do next.

FPGA servo PWM amplitude gauge

ELEC5566M group project, University of Leeds · Verilog on a Cyclone V DE1-SoC · ModelSim, Quartus

My subsystem within a four-person FPGA signal visualiser: a Verilog PWM controller that drives an SG90 servo as a physical amplitude gauge. Every number is derived from a constraint: a 20 ms servo frame at 50 MHz is 1,000,000 ticks, which needs a 20-bit counter, with four gain positions mapped evenly from 50,000 to 100,000 ticks. Simulated in ModelSim, then validated on real hardware through the LT24 adapter, cross-checking the arm position against the seven-segment display.

Code and full design derivation: github.com/jelilerosajose00/fpga-servo-pwm-gauge
